## 1.5 SPD- Matrices
**Def.:** A matrix $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ is SPD, if
- $A$ is selfadjoint: $A=A^H$ (symmetric for $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}$).
- $A$ is positive definite: $\forall x \in \mathbb{K}^n \setminus \{ 0 \}: <Ax,x>_{2}>0$.

**Lemma:**
Let $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n\times n}$ be SPD.
Then $A$ is regular and $A_{jj}>0$ for all $j \in \{ 1,\dots,n \}$, ie. Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel are well-definied.

*Proof:*
$\text{ker}A =\{ 0 \}$, since $A$ is positive definite. Hence, $A$ is regular.
$A_{jj }= <Ae_{j},e_{j}> >0$.

**Lemma:** Let $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ be SPD. Then $<x,y>_{A}:= <Ax,y>$ definies a scalar product on $\mathbb{K}^n$ with induced norm $||x||_{A}:=<x,x>_{A}^{1/2}$, the so-called energy norm.

*Proof:* Clearly $<.,.>_{A}$ is definite, as $x \neq 0$yields $<x,x>_{A}=<Ax,x>_{2}>0$.
Clearly, $<.,.>_{A}$ is linear in the second argument.
It remains to prove antisymmetry. 
$<x,y>_{A}=<Ax,y>_{2}= <x,A^Hy>_{2}=<x,Ay>_{2}=\overline{<Ay,x>_{2}}= \overline{<y,x>_{A}}$.

**Recall:** The SPD matrix $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ is diagonalizable, i.e., ex. $\Lambda = \text{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})$ and $Q \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$s.t.
- $A = Q \Lambda Q^T$, $Q^HQ=I$
- $\sigma(A)=\sigma(\Lambda)=\{ \lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n} \}\subseteq \mathbb{R}_{>0}$.

**Def.:**  $A^{\pm 1/2}=Q\Lambda^{\pm 1/2}Q^H$ with $\Lambda^{\pm 1/2}:=\text{diag}(\lambda_{1}^{\pm 1/2},\dots,\lambda_{n}^{\pm 1/2})$.

**Clear:** $A^{1/2}A^{-1/2}=I$, $A^{-1/2}A=A^{1/2}$.

**Notation:** Let $A,B \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$be self andjoint. We write $A >B$ if $A-B$ is SPD.

**Lemma:** Let $A,B \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ be selfadjoint. Then, there holds:
1. $A>0 \iff$ $\forall C \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ regular: $C^HAC>0$.
2. $A > B$ $\implies$ $\forall C \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ regular: $C^HAC>C^HBC$.
3. $A,B >0$ $\implies$ $A+B>0$
4. $\lambda_{min}I<A<\lambda_{max}I \iff \sigma(A)\subseteq (\lambda_{min},\lambda_{max})$.
5. $A>B>0 \iff B^{-1}>A^{-1}>0$.

*Proof:* The second statement directly follows from the first.
(i).  It suffices to prove "$\implies$" (because $C^{-1}$ is regular and $(C^{-1})^H=(C^H)^{-1}=:C^{-H}$). Let $A>0$. Let $x \neq 0 \implies Cx\stackrel{C \text{ regular}}{\neq}0$. $<C^HACx,x>_{2}=<ACx,Cx>_{2}>0$.

(iv). Since $A$ is selfadjoint. There exists $\{ v_{1},\dots,v_{n} \} \subset \mathbb{K}^n$ basis such thath
- $Av_{j}=\lambda_{j}v_{j}$
- $<v_{j},v_{k}>_{2}=\delta_{jk}$
- $\lambda_{1}<\dots<\lambda_{n}$.
**Recall:** If $x=\sum_{j=1}^n\mu_{j}v_{j}$, then $||x||^3_{2}=\sum_{j=1}^n|\mu_{j}|^2$, $<Ax,x>_{2}=\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda_{j}|\mu_{j}|^2$.
$\implies <(A-\lambda_{min}I)x,x>_{2}=<Ax,x>-\lambda_{min} ||x||_{2}^2 \stackrel{}{=}\sum_{j}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{min})|\mu_{j}|^2$,
i.e. LHS $>0$ $\iff$ $\sigma(A)\subset(\lambda_{min},+\infty)$
Analogously: $A<\lambda_{max}I \iff \sigma(A)\subset(-\infty,\lambda_{max})$.

(v). It suffices to prove "$\implies$" (as $(X^-1)^{-1}=X$).
If $X$ is SPDm then $Xv=\lambda v \iff \frac{1}{\lambda}v=X^{-1}v$.
Hence, $A>0$ yields $A^{-1}>0$.
Moreover, $A>B$ yields $X:=B^{-1/2}AB^{-1/2}\stackrel{\text{(ii)}}{>}B^{-1/2}BB^{-1/2}=I$ (root of $B$/invers root is welldefinied, are also SPD), so this matrix is SPD. 
$\implies \sigma(X)\subset(1,+\infty)$ (Because $>I$).
$\implies \sigma(X^{-1})\subset(0,1)$.
So $B^{1/2}A^{-1}B^{1/2}=X^{-1}<I$ $\implies$ $A^{-1}=B^{-1/2}XB^{-1/2}<B^{-1/2}B^{-1/2}=B^{-1}$.

**Theorem:** (Convergence of damped Jacobi)
Let $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ be SPD, $D \in \mathbb{n \times n}$ defined by $D_{jk}:=A_{jj}\delta_{jk}>0$.
For $M^{(J)}:=I-\omega D^{-1}A$ be the iteration matrix of the damped Jacobi iteration.
Then, $\rho(M_{\omega}^{(J)})=||A^{1/2}M_{\omega}^{(J)}A^{1/2}||_{2}=||M_{\omega}^{(J)}||_{A}$.
Moreover,  it holds, that $||M_{\omega}^{(J)}||_{A}<1 \iff A <\frac{2}{\omega}D$.

In particular, we get contraction w.r.t energy norm in each step of the iteration, so no preasymptotic phase.
If $Ax^*=b$, then $||x^*-x_{k+1}||_{A}\leq \underbrace{||M_{\omega}^{(J)}}_{<1}||_{A}||x^*-x_{k}||_{A}$.
*The iff tells us we NEED damping, so we need the diagonal and compute the energy norm... unsatisfactory.*

*Proof:*
**Step 1:** For every $M \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$, $x \in \mathbb{K}^n$, $y:=A^{1/2}x$, there holds $||x||_{A}^2 = <Ax,x>_{2}=<A^{1/2}x,A^{1/2}x>_{2}=||y||^2_{2}$ and $||Mx||_{A}^2= || A^{1/2}Mx||_{2}^2=||A^{1/2}M A^{-1/2}y||_{2}$
$\implies ||M||_{A}= \text{sup}_{x\neq 0} \frac{||Mx||_{A}}{||x||_{A}}= \text{sup}_{y \neq 0} \frac{||A^{1/2}MA^{-1/2}y||_{2}}{||y||_{2}}=||A^{1/2}MA^{-1/2}||_{2}$.
**Step 2:** Let $M = M_{\omega}^{(J)}$. $A^{1/2}M A^{-1/2}=I -\omega A^{1/2}DA^{1/2}$ is selfadjoint!
$\underbrace{\rho(A^{1/2}MA^{-1/2})}_{=\rho(M)}=||A^{1/2}MA^{-1/2}||_{2}$ as $\sigma(M)=\sigma(A^{1/2}MA^{-1/2})$.
**Step 3:** We have to show 
$$ 
\begin{align}
\rho(M)<1 &\iff \sigma(A^{1/2}MA^{-1/2})\subset(-1,1)\\
&\iff -I<I-\omega A^{1/2}MD^{-1}A^{1/2}<I  \\
&\iff -2I<-\omega A^{1/2}D^{-1}A^{1/2}<2I  \\
&\iff 0<A^{1/2}D^{-1}A^{1/2}< \frac{2}{\omega}I  \\
&\iff 0<D^{-1}< \frac{2}{\Omega}A^{-1} \\
&\iff 0< \frac{\omega}{2}A<D
\end{align}.
$$
**Recall:** The Gauss-Seidel method reads
$$
x_{i}^{k+1}:=x_{i}^k + \frac{1}{A_{ii}}\left( b_{i}- \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} A_{ij}x_{j}^{k+1} -\sum_{j=i}^nA_{ij}x_{j}^k\right) \quad \text{ for all } i=1,\dots,n.
$$.
We split $A = L + D +U$ disjointly (lower, diagonal, upper). 
Multiply equation by $A_{ii}$, the
$$
\begin{align}
Dx_{k+1}&=Dx_{k}+ (b-L x_{k+1}-(D+U)x_{k}) \\
&= (D+L)x_{k}+ (b-Ax_{k})-Lx_{k+1}
\end{align}
$$
$$
\begin{align}
&\iff (D+L)x_{k+1}=(D+L)x_{k}+(b-A x_{k}) \\
&\iff x_{k+1}=x_{k}+(D+L)^{-1}(b-A_{x_{k}})
\end{align}
$$
So $M^{(GS)}:= I-(D+L)^{-1}A$

Analogously, SOR does 
$$
x_{i}^{k+1}:=x_{i}^k + \frac{\omega}{A_{ii}}\left( b_{i}- \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} A_{ij}x_{j}^{k+1} -\sum_{j=i}^nA_{ij}x_{j}^k\right) \quad \text{ for all } i=1,\dots,n.
$$$$
\begin{align}
Dx_{k+1}&=Dx_{k}+ \omega(b-L x_{k+1}-(D+U)x_{k}) \\
&= (D+\omega L)x_{k}+ \omega(b-Ax_{k})-\omega Lx_{k+1}
\end{align}
$$$$
\begin{align}
&\iff (D+\omega L)x_{k+1}=(D+\omega L)x_{k}+\omega(b-A x_{k}) \\
&\iff x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\omega(D+\omega L)^{-1}(b-A_{x_{k}})
\end{align}
$$
$M_{\omega}^{(SOR)}=I-\omega(D+\omega L)^{-1}A$
$\omega =1 \implies M_{\omega}^{(SOR)}=M^{(GS)}$.

Independently of the Matrix, for every $\omega \in(0,2)$, we get convergence!
SOR ... successive over-relaxation
*In practice, choosin $\omega >1$ yiels faster convergence :) *

**Theorem:** (Convergence of SOR)
Let $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ be SPD. Let $0<\omega<2$. Let $D,L\in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ be defined by 
$$D_{jk}=
\begin{cases}
A_{jj} \quad \text{ if } j=k \\
0 \quad \text{ else }
\end{cases}, \qquad
L_{jk}=\begin{cases}
A_{jk} \quad \text{ if } j>k \\
0 \quad \text{ else }
\end{cases}
$$
Let $M_{\omega}^{SOR}:= I-\omega(D+\omega L)^{-1}A \implies \rho(M_{\omega}^{SOR})\leq ||M_{\omega}^{SOR}||_{A}<1$ i.e. SOR (and Gauss-Seidel) always lead to convergence.

*Proof:* 
**Step 1:** Denote $M:=I-W^{-1}A$, $W=\frac{1}{\omega}(D+\omega L)=\frac{1}{\omega}D +L$.
$||Mx||_{A}^2 =< AMx,Mx>_{2}=<M^H A Mx,x>_{2}$.
$$
\begin{align}
M^H A M &= (I - AW^{-H})A(I-W^{-1}A) \\
&=A-AW^{-H}A-A W^{-1}A+AW^{-H}AW^{-1}A \\
&= A-A(\underbrace{W^{-H}+W^{-1}-W^{-H}AW^{-1}}_{W^{-H}(W+W^H-A)W^{-1}=:S})A \\
\end{align}
$$
Suppose that $S > 0$-
$\implies ||Mx||_{A}=||x||_{A}- \underbrace{<ASAx,x>_{2}}_{=<S(AX),Ax>_{2}>0}$.
$\implies ||Mx||_{A}<||x||_{A}$
$\implies||M||_{A}<1$ (??Warum strikt??)

**Step 2:** $S>0 \iff W+W^H-A >0$.
$$
W+W^H-A = \left( \frac{1}{\omega}D+L \right) +\left( \frac{1}{\omega}D+L^H \right)-(L+D+L^H) = (\frac{2}{\omega}-1)D
$$
Because $D$ is positive definite: $\iff \frac{2}{\omega}-1>0 \iff 0<\omega<2$.

One drawback of SOR ist, that $M_{\omega}^{SOR}$ is non-selfadjoint, even if $A$ is SPD.
We cure that by symmetrization and obtain SSOR (=symmetrized SOR).

First, we do one step with (forward) SOR (thats the one we have).
$$
\tilde{x}_{k+1}=x_{k}+ \omega(D+\omega L)^{-1}(b-Ax_{k})
$$
so $M_{\omega}^{SOR}:= I- \omega(D+\omega L)^{-1}A$.

This is arbitrary, because we could also do it from below (then we'd get the U part everywhere).
So then, we don one step of backward SOR:
$$
x_{k+1}:=\bar{x}_{k+1} + \omega(D+\omega U)^{-1}(b-A \tilde{x}_{k+1})
$$
So $\bar{M}^{SOR}_{\omega}:=I-\omega(D+\omega U)^{-1}A$
$\implies M_{\omega}^{SSOR}:=\bar{M}_{\omega}^{SOR}M_{\omega}^{SOR}$.

**Theorem:** (Convergence of SSOR)
Let $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ be SPD, $\omega \in(0,2)$.
$\implies \rho(M_{\omega}^{SSOR})=||M_{\omega}^{SSOR}||_{A}=||M_{\omega}^{SOR}||_{A}^2<1$

*Because of the equality, we always choose the right contraction factor, not just asymptotic.*

*Proof:* 
**Step 1:** For $M \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$, define $M^A:=A^{-1}M^HA$ $\implies <M^Ax,y>_{A}=<A(A^{-1}M^HA)x,y>_{2} = <M^HAx,y>_{2}= <Ax,M^Hy>_{2}=<x,My>_{A}$, so $M^A$ is the adjoint of $M$ w.r.t $<.,.>_{A}$.

**Step 2:** Consider $M:=M_{\omega}^{SOR}=I-\omega(D+\omega L)^{-1}A$.
Then $M^H=I-\omega A(D+\omega L^H)^{-1}$.
$\implies M^A= A^{-1}M^HA=I-\omega(D+\omega \underbrace{L^H)}_{=U}^{-1}A=\bar{M}_{\omega}^{SOR}$.
$\implies M_{\omega}^{SSOR}=M^AM$ is self-adjoined w.r.t. $<.,.>_{A}$
In particular, $\sigma(M^AM)\subset \mathbb{R}$.
Moreover, $<M^AMx,x>_{A}=<Mx,Mx>_{A}=||Mx||_{A}^2 \geq0$.
So $\sigma(M^AM)\subset \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ and $\rho(M^AM)=||M^AM||_{A}$.

Finally, $||M||_{A}^2 = \text{sup}_{x \neq 0} \frac{||Mx||_{A}^2}{||x||_{A}}=\text{sup}_{x\neq 0} \frac{<M^AMx,x>_{A}}{||x||_{A}}=\rho(M^A M)$ because this is exactly the Rayleigh-coefficient.

From the previous theorem, we know that $||M||_{A}<1 \iff 0<\omega<2$.

**Remark:** A laborious, but elementary calculation shows
$$
M_{\omega}^{SSOR}=I-\omega(2-\omega)(D+\omega U)^{-1}D(D+\omega L)^{-1}A
$$

## 2.1. Preliminaries
**Lemma:** (Krylov spaces) (these guys are smart guys)
Let $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ be regular, $b \in \mathbb{K}^n \setminus \{ 0 \}$, $x^* \in \mathbb{K}^n\setminus \{ 0 \}$ with $Ax^*=0$.
For $\ell \in \mathbb{N}$, define $\mathcal{K}_{\ell}:=\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(A,b):=\text{span}(b,Ab,\dots,A^\ell b)$.
Then, the following ist equivalent:
1. $\text{dim}\mathcal{K}\ell\leq \ell$
2.  $\mathcal{K}_{\ell}=\mathcal{K}_{\ell+1}$
3. $A(\mathcal{K}_{\ell})\subset\mathcal{K}_{\ell}$
4. $x^* \in \mathcal{K}_{\ell}$
In particular, there exists a minimal $1\leq \ell^*\leq n$ with $x^* \in \mathcal{K}_{\ell}$.

*Proof:*
(i $\implies$ii): Choose $m\leq \ell$ minimal s.t. $\{ b,Ab,\dots,A^mb \}$ is linearily dependent.
$\implies$ there exists $\mu_{j}$ s.t. $0=\sum_{j=0}^m\mu_{j}A^jb$ and $\mu_{m}\neq 0$.
$\implies A^mb=-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{m}}A^jb$
$\implies A^\ell b=A^{\ell-m}(A^m b)=-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{m}}A^{\ell-m +j}b \in \mathcal{K}_{\ell}$.
$\implies \mathcal{K_{\ell+1}}\subset\mathcal{K_{\ell}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\ell +1}}.$

(ii $\implies$iii): $A(A^Jb)=A^{j+1}b \in \mathcal{K_{\ell+1}}=\mathcal{K}_{\ell}$.
$\implies A:\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\to \mathcal{K}_{\ell}$ with $\text{ker}(A)=\{ 0 \}$.
$\implies$ bijective, $b \in\mathcal{K}_{\ell}, x^*=A^{-1}b \in \mathcal{K}_{\ell}$.

(iv $\implies$i): $x^* \in \mathcal{K}_{\ell}=\text{span}(b,Ab,\dots,A^{\ell-1} b)$
$\implies Ax^*=b \in \text{span}(Ab,A^2b,\dots,A^\ell b) \implies \text{dim}\mathcal{K}_{\ell+1}\leq \ell$.
